01 — Introduction

Who am I?

I am a Professor in the School of Electrical and Computer Engineering and the Department of Computer Science at Purdue University, in the sleepy and charming university town of West Lafayette, Indiana. I am the Co‑founder and CTO of a cloud computing startup, KeyByte. I have had the joy of graduating 27 PhD students and 30 Masters thesis students from my group, who are all in various stages of building wonderful careers in academia and industry.

I was born and grew up in the overwhelming, contradiction‑filled, warm, embracing city of Calcutta (renamed Kolkata) in India. With my father's job as a civil engineer in the Federal Government of India, I had the fortune — though it did not seem that great at the time — of traveling through many parts of India. I did my undergraduate studies at the Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, in Computer Science, and after that, following a hallowed and well‑worn path, I came to the US for MS and PhD. I landed in the sleepy and charming university towns of Urbana and Champaign to do my studies at the University of Illinois at Urbana‑Champaign. Four short years later, I added a "Dr." to my name and was out into the bright lights of industry, starting work at the IBM T. J. Watson Research Center in New York.

A decade and a half later, I am still idealistic, still wonder‑struck that a profession can be so rewarding.

A few years of learning from — and then working together with — some of the greats of computer science at IBM, the academic bug bit me, and I headed back to the mid‑west to become an idealistic Assistant Professor at Purdue University. A decade and a half later, I am still idealistic, still wonder‑struck that a profession can be so rewarding as I teach the next generation of scientists and engineers, do research with the best and the brightest, and learn so much in the process. One scenic detour has been a successful startup I did with three colleagues, SensorHound Innovations, on embedded system reliability. Currently at Purdue, I lead the National Science Foundation center CHORUS (2024–current) on resilient cyber‑physical systems, and the Army's Artificial Intelligence Innovation Institute (A2I2) (2020–current) on assured autonomous operations.

Our technical community has been generous with awards, acknowledging our technical and societal contributions. Our work has received 13 best paper or runner‑up for best paper awards, and two Test‑of‑Time awards (DSN 2022; Middleware 2025). I am a nominated member of the International Federation for Information Processing (IFIP), a Fellow of the Institute of Engineering and Technology (IET) (2022) and of the Asia Pacific Artificial Intelligence Association (AAIA) (2025), and recipient of the Humboldt Award (2018).

IET Fellow · 2022 AAIA Fellow · 2025 Humboldt Award · 2018 IFIP Nominated Member 13 best paper / runner-up awards 2 Test-of-Time awards

On a personal level, I maintain strong connections to India, serving as International Visiting Faculty at IIT Kharagpur and IIT Bombay. I have been a 20+ year volunteer with Asha for Education, a non‑profit that has been supporting basic education for underprivileged children in India since 1991. I love to play tennis — surface no matter — and for those snowy days in the Midwest, pickleball would do equally well. My wife is also a Professor at Purdue, and she has kept me sane (to some extent), on my toes (especially when she zips past me during our bike rides), energetic (whenever I feel like lolling too much, she lets me know), and fun (no professorial airs, please).

Same habit in every room I'm in — from the classroom to the boardroom: sketch it out, then build it.

02 — Platform

My vision and plans for our technical community

IEEE Computer Society stands at an inflection point — poised not just to disseminate technical knowledge, but to be a beacon showing our community the path forward. My two terms on the CS Board of Governors, along with my current leadership of two Society‑wide programs — CS Juniors and the Global Student Challenge — have prepared me to lead CS toward a future in which we shape computing technology as a powerful force for serving humanity.

I.

Academia–industry–government collaboration

While on the Board of Governors, I led a successful effort to make CS the locus for academia‑industry‑government collaboration. CS is uniquely positioned to catalyze this, but too often we've missed the calling. I will change this through focused programs: joint ventures in roadmapping technology sectors; standards development to ensure interoperability and security; joint prototype creation; and student challenge competitions that build effective talent pipelines.

II.

Elevate Society members

CS must proactively nurture students and young professionals (SYP) to become leaders. Currently leading CS Juniors and the Global Student Challenge, I have a deep appreciation for the role of SYP. I will streamline our operations and create clear pathways for our global members — far beyond our boardrooms — to contribute to, and then lead, important programs.

III.

An independent voice in technology policy

I will energize IEEE CS to become a more trusted independent voice in technology matters. Policymakers struggle to keep pace with technological change, leaving them reliant on biased lobbyists. We will create standing, rapid‑response task forces of domain experts who can translate complex research into accessible policy briefs, and train our members to amplify their impact through path‑defining contributions and greater cooperation with other societies (ACM, IFIP), policymakers, and standards bodies (NIST, IETF, ISO).

IV.

Efficient operation

I will make CS agile and cost‑efficient in initiating and sustaining activities worldwide — leading through local, member‑driven events that tap into the vast passion of our members to contribute, given the proper support and recognition. Throughout, I will make these events broadly accessible across financial and geographical lines.

This moment of opportunity excites me. Drawing on my leadership experience across IEEE, ACM, and IFIP, as well as my passion and discipline, I am committed to making the Society a deeply rewarding professional home for its members and a powerful, enduring force for good in the broader world.

03 — Record

What have I done so far?

2021 — current

Chair, Computer Society Student Challenge Competition

The challenge competitions give students a platform to build innovative solutions to real‑world problems, using real‑world datasets from academic and industry teams. They alternate between the global student community and the North America‑based community. The last Global Student Competition drew 500 registered students; the last North America Competition (2024) drew 43 teams. Each was financially sponsored by IEEE CS and private industry, organized with two other Board member colleagues (Prof. Deborah Silver and Prof. Joaquim Jorge), with the final held in person at an IEEE CS conference.

2024 — 2026

Chair (2026), Co‑Chair (2025), Member (2024) — IEEE CS Juniors Program

A global program, started in 2024, meant to inspire K‑12 students to explore and excel in computing activities — sparking curiosity in computer science and building lasting relationships with students, educators, and communities. As (Co‑)Chair, my responsibilities have included defining the rubric for project selection, disseminating program information widely to attract deserving proposals, coordinating proposal evaluation, and advising organizers on successful event execution.

2017–20, 2022–24

Computer Society Board of Governors Member

I took on various participatory and leadership roles during my stints on the board, focused on two overarching goals — energizing students and young professionals, and creating synergies between academic and industry professionals. Specific roles included leading student activities within the Geographical Activities Committee (GAC), IEEE CS representative to the IEEE Biometrics Council, Charter Member of the CS Distinguished Contributor Recognition Program, and member of the Ad‑Hoc Committee on ideation of novel CS activities.

2021 — 2024

Distinguished Visitor, IEEE Computer Society

Selected as a Distinguished Visitor, I gave 6 talks — including two keynotes — at campuses (IISc Bangalore, University of Lisbon, University of Glasgow, and others) and CS conferences (keynotes at ISSRE, InC4). Topics included "Turning Padawans to Jedi Knights: Best practices for training our computing students," "How to have a Terrific or a Terrible Life During Your First Three Years as Faculty," and "Dependability and Data Analytics: A Match Made in the Cloud."

8× in 10 years

Steering Committee & Organizing Committee, TCFTC and DSN Conference

A core member of the IEEE CS Technical Community on Dependable Computing and Fault Tolerance (TCFTC), I have served on the Organizing Committee of its primary conference, Dependable Systems and Networks (DSN), 8 times in the last 10 years — including as Founder of the Rising Star Award (2020), Workshop Chair (2021, 2025), Steering Committee (2018–19), and PC Chair (2011).
